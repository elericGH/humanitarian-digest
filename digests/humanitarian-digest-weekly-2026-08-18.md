# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 11 August 2026 – 18 August 2026
*Produced Tuesday, 18 August 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Australia's aid sector spent the week focused on two fronts: the Chocó earthquake response in Colombia and continuing scrutiny of the 2026-27 aid budget. DFAT's ministerial office has been active on humanitarian assistance announcements in recent weeks (most recently a Horn of Africa package and a Sudan conflict response), and the Australian Council for International Development (ACFID) continues to track sector-wide funding pressures, noting the 2026-27 total Australian ODA budget estimate sits at $741.7 million. No new dedicated DFAT humanitarian announcement was published in the past seven days specifically referencing the Colombia earthquake or South Sudan funding crisis, though both are highly likely candidates for a forthcoming Australian response given past patterns of DFAT engagement in comparable disasters.

The Emergency Action Alliance, which unites 15 Australian charities including ADRA, Act for Peace, Caritas Australia, Oxfam Australia, Save the Children Australia and others under a coordinated appeal mechanism, has not issued a new activation this week; its most recent live appeal remains tied to the Myanmar earthquake response from earlier in the year. Given the scale of the Colombia earthquake (see Tier 3 below) and the deepening South Sudan refugee funding gap (see Tier 2), member agencies may activate a joint appeal in the coming days.

ACFID's public commentary this week reiterated concerns raised in its State of the Sector reporting about Australian NGOs adapting to volatile donor priorities while sustaining accountability standards across concurrent crises in Myanmar, the Horn of Africa, Sudan and now Colombia.

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

The Pacific and Southeast Asia faced an active week of tropical cyclone activity. The Pacific Disaster Center's DisasterAWARE tracker recorded multiple simultaneous systems as of 15 August, including Tropical Cyclone 12W (Dolphin), Tropical Cyclone 14W (Chan-hom) and Tropical Cyclone 13W (Kujira) moving through the western Pacific, alongside Tropical Cyclone 08E (Hernan) and Tropical Cyclone 01C (Lala) further east. Guam and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands braced for a second super typhoon impact in under three months, underscoring the compounding disaster burden facing US-affiliated Pacific territories this season.

In the Philippines, the combined effects of an enhanced Southwest Monsoon, Tropical Depression Luis and Tropical Cyclone Kujira triggered widespread flooding, landslides and strong winds in early August, affecting an estimated 586,000 people and displacing roughly 32,800. The AHA Centre's ASEAN Weekly Disaster Update for Week 32 (3–9 August) logged the regional flooding and monsoon impacts across Indonesia and the wider ASEAN bloc.

Myanmar's monsoon season compounded an already severe humanitarian crisis this week: WFP launched an emergency flood response on 13 August after rapid assessments found more than 32,000 people across 98 villages and five wards affected in Ayeyarwady Region. WFP dispatched 20 metric tonnes of fortified biscuits to local partners, enough to reach roughly 10,000 people, a stopgap measure given the scale of need.

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

Gaza's humanitarian situation remained critical through the reporting week. OCHA's oPt situation reports (7 and 14 August) recorded continuing military activity, with nearly the entire population of 2.1 million concentrated in less than half of the Strip under extremely overcrowded conditions. Since the 10 October 2025 ceasefire announcement, 1,254 Palestinians have been killed, 4,121 injured and 804 retrieved from rubble as of 5 August. WHO has recorded 45 attacks on health care in Gaza so far in 2026. Al Jazeera's coverage this week highlighted the toll on Gaza's children, with more than 58,000 having lost one or both parents, and warned that famine conditions could return even as children are still recovering from the 2025 famine. In the West Bank, a Jerusalem District Court injunction on 3 August temporarily suspended demolitions in Masafer Yatta communities, protecting roughly 160 structures and 400 residents, a rare bright spot amid a demolition rate at least twice the 15-year average since 2024.

South Sudan's refugee crisis sharpened dramatically this week. A joint WFP/UNHCR release (14 August) warned that more than 650,000 refugees and asylum seekers risk losing assistance due to funding shortfalls, with 240,000 of the most vulnerable set to be cut from food and nutrition support within weeks. WFP faces an immediate US$37 million gap for its refugee response and a US$258 million shortfall for the remainder of 2026; UNHCR has secured only 28% of the $286 million it needs this year. Without new funding, no refugees in South Sudan will receive food assistance from October. The crisis is compounded by more than 1.4 million refugees and returnees who have crossed into South Sudan since Sudan's war began in 2023.

Sudan's own displacement crisis continued to deepen. A UN News report (mid-August) documented fighting in West Darfur displacing roughly 18,000 people from more than 20 villages in the past week, most fleeing across the border into Chad, while RSF forces encircled El Obeid, capital of North Kordofan, threatening a full-scale assault. Funding shortfalls forced the closure of three health facilities in Central Darfur, cutting off an estimated 75,000 displaced people from care. Sudan's US$3 billion humanitarian appeal remains only 40% funded, with $1.2 billion received so far this year. The ICRC facilitated the handover of 15 people released by Congolese authorities to the AFC/M23 alliance in the DRC between 6 and 7 August under the Doha Mechanism, one of the few concrete protection outcomes reported this week in the eastern DRC conflict.

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

The dominant global story this week was the magnitude 7.4–7.5 earthquake that struck Chocó department in western Colombia on 10 August. Death tolls climbed through the week, with reports ranging from 111 (initial NPR reporting) to 281–294 confirmed dead, more than 4,100 injured, and over 140 people still missing as of mid-week reporting. More than 12,000 homes were destroyed. The quake was felt across western and central Colombia and into neighbouring Ecuador and Panama, with significant damage reported in Cali, Pereira, Quibdó and Manizales; Cali's mayor reported 32 building collapses, including part of a hospital. Direct Relief mobilised medical aid within days of the quake, and Colombia's government declared a national emergency with search-and-rescue operations ongoing.

Latin America and the Caribbean more broadly saw continued situation monitoring via ReliefWeb's weekly regional updates (7 and 14 August), reflecting the sustained multi-hazard load facing the region alongside the Colombia response.

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

The Colombia earthquake (magnitude 7.4–7.5, 10 August, Chocó department) is the week's most significant sudden-onset hazard, with a still-rising death toll and major structural damage across multiple cities. Concurrently, GDACS recorded a green-alert earthquake in Indonesia on 31 July continuing to generate aftershock monitoring into the reporting week. Tropical cyclone activity remained elevated across both the western Pacific (Dolphin, Chan-hom, Kujira) and eastern Pacific/Atlantic basins (Hernan, Lala, and Atlantic invests 92W/94L), with Guam and the CNMI on alert for a second super typhoon in under three months. Philippine monsoon-and-cyclone flooding (Tropical Depression Luis plus Typhoon Kujira) displaced roughly 32,800 people and affected 586,000 in the first half of August.
