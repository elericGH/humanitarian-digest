# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 3 August 2026 – 10 August 2026
*Produced Monday, 10 August 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Australia and UNHCR announced A$25.25 million in multiyear funding (2026–28) for Rohingya refugees and Bangladeshi host communities on 3 August. Australian High Commissioner Susan Ryle said the flexible contribution would support protection work, essential services, skills-development programmes and disaster-risk reduction, with a particular focus on women, girls and people with disabilities. This builds on Australia's existing commitments to the Rohingya response and comes as the broader refugee crisis remains one of the world's most protracted displacement situations. ([DFAT/Migrant Times](https://migrantimes.com/human-rights-welfare/05-08-2026/australia-unhcr-announce-a2525-million-for-rohingya-aid))

ACFID launched its State of the Sector Report 2026 on 6 August, presented by Andrew Leigh, offering a comprehensive picture of Australian development NGOs across 36 indicators. The report lands against a backdrop in which Australia's international development budget has grown to a record $5.1 billion (up $135 million on last year) even as ODA as a share of GNI and of the federal budget sits at its lowest-ever level, underscoring the tension between headline funding increases and real-terms erosion of Australia's aid effort. ([ACFID](https://acfid.asn.au/))

No new DFAT ministerial humanitarian announcements beyond the Rohingya package were identified in the past seven days; DFAT's most recent releases on Gaza, Myanmar and the Horn of Africa predate this reporting window but remain part of the active response portfolio. ([DFAT Ministers](https://ministers.dfat.gov.au/minister/anne-aly/media-release/further-humanitarian-assistance-gaza))

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

The Pacific Disaster Center's DisasterAWARE tropical cyclone snapshot (8 August) showed active systems across the North Pacific: Tropical Cyclone 12W (Dolphin), Tropical Cyclone 14W (Chan-hom), the remnants of former Tropical Cyclone 13W, and an area of interest, Invest 98W, being monitored for further development. None were confirmed as directly threatening populated Pacific Island states as of this writing, but the cluster of simultaneous systems points to an active mid-year cyclone season in the region. ([PDC](https://www.pdc.org/weather-wall/))

This follows closely on the heels of Guam and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands' recovery from Super Typhoon Bavi in early July — the second super typhoon to strike the territories within roughly 83 days, after Typhoon Sinlaku earlier in the year. Recovery work (downed power infrastructure, damaged water systems on Rota, and FEMA-supported debris clearance) is ongoing and will likely continue to compete for regional response capacity if new storms develop this week. ([PDC](https://www.pdc.org/guam-cnmi-suffer-impacts-from-second-super-typhoon-in-just-83-days/))

Regional commentary continued to emphasise the Pacific's more assertive posture heading through 2026, with island states pushing for development and security partnerships that respect sovereignty and prioritise climate justice ahead of October's Pacific-hosted climate meeting in Tuvalu. Analysts also flagged persistent major-power flashpoints across the wider Asia-Pacific — from the Korean Peninsula to the Taiwan Strait to the South China Sea — as a live backdrop to humanitarian and disaster-response coordination in the region. ([East Asia Forum](https://eastasiaforum.org/2026/); [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/tag/pacific-islands-climate-change/))

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

Gaza's humanitarian situation remains acute. OCHA's latest Occupied Palestinian Territory reporting describes continued military operations, fresh displacement and constrained aid access, with communities reporting persistent fear tied to shelling near displacement sites. A fourth round of the Gaza Joint WASH Assessment found a net decline in household water and sanitation access between August 2025 and March 2026: 63% of assessed households now access less than six litres of drinking water per person per day. Engine-oil shortages continue to threaten sewage systems, and UNRWA says it remains unable to distribute pre-positioned food, flour and shelter supplies for hundreds of thousands of people due to continued access restrictions from Israeli authorities. ReliefWeb's Lebanon Response Situation Report #9 (5 August) tracks the parallel Middle East conflict response in Lebanon. ([OCHA oPt](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-16-july-2026); [ReliefWeb Lebanon](https://reliefweb.int/report/lebanon/middle-east-conflict-lebanon-response-situation-report-9-august-5-2026))

Sudan's crisis deepened further this week. The UN Security Council held live warnings of famine and state collapse as South Sudan's peace deal frays, compounding an already catastrophic picture in Sudan proper: nearly 19.5 million people face acute food insecurity, with roughly 135,000 people in catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) conditions across 14 Darfur and South Kordofan hotspots. Sudan's 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan is only about 41% funded ($1.2 billion of $2.9 billion required), and WFP food assistance has fallen 14% since the start of the year for lack of financing. Central African Republic Situation Report No. 79 (4 August) also points to continued instability spilling across the wider region. ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168089); [WFP Sudan](https://www.wfp.org/emergencies/sudan); [ReliefWeb CAR](https://reliefweb.int/report/central-african-republic/central-african-republic-situation-report-no-79-4-august-2026))

WFP's new Global Outlook mid-year update warns that the 2026–27 El Niño cycle could push at least 49 million more people into acute food insecurity by the end of 2027, lifting the total from roughly 225 million to 274 million — a 22% increase. Sudan, South Sudan, Yemen and Palestine remain the highest-concern hotspots, with northeast Nigeria and Somalia newly flagged as deteriorating toward catastrophic levels. ([WFP](https://www.wfp.org/news/new-fao-wfp-report-warns-worsening-hunger-puts-13-hotspots-significant-risk))

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

Venezuela's earthquake response continues to dominate the "rest of world" picture. Government figures released 3 August put the death toll from the June quakes at 6,125, with nearly 61,000 hospitalised; more than 16,300 people lost their homes outright and a further 28,300 have homes rendered uninhabitable. As of 3 August, La Guaira's governor said 1,579 people remained missing, though other estimates vary substantially. UNHCR notes the quake struck a population already living through one of the world's largest displacement crises, compounding an already fragile humanitarian baseline. ReliefWeb's Venezuela Earthquake Response Situation Reports #19 and #32 (31 July–7 August) track the ongoing response. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/death-toll-from-venezuela-earthquakes-passes-6000); [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/venezuela-bolivarian-republic/earthquakes-venezuela-situation-report-32-07-august-2026-time-0200-pm))

WHO's Situation Report No. 3 on flash floods in Afghanistan (August 2026) and Islamic Relief's Pakistan situation report (covering 26 June–5 August) both point to an active South Asian monsoon season straining already-stretched humanitarian systems. Separately, UNHCR's mid-year data shows global refugee numbers fell 3% in 2025 to 41.6 million — the first decline in a decade — though 117.8 million people (one in 70 globally) remain forcibly displaced overall, with 14.7 million people returning home in 2025 under conditions that are often far from safe or sustainable, particularly in Afghanistan, Sudan and Syria. ([ReliefWeb Afghanistan](https://reliefweb.int/report/afghanistan/who-situation-report-no-3-flash-floods-afghanistan-august-2026); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/06/1167693))

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

Multiple tropical cyclones are active or forming across the North Pacific this week — 12W (Dolphin), 14W (Chan-hom) and remnants of 13W, plus Invest 98W under watch — per PDC's 8 August tracker, in a region still recovering from consecutive super typhoons earlier this year. ReliefWeb's Global Weather Hazards Summary for 6–12 August flags the wider window of concern. Venezuela's earthquake death toll continued to be revised upward this week (6,125 as of 3 August), underlining that "sudden onset" impacts often take months to fully account for. No new GDACS Red or Orange alerts specific to this seven-day window were identified from publicly available summaries. ([PDC](https://www.pdc.org/weather-wall/); [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/global-weather-hazards-summary-august-6-2026-august-12-2026))
