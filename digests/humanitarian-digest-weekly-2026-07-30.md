# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 23 July 2026 – 30 July 2026
*Produced Thursday, 30 July 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

The Lowy Institute published its [2026 Pacific Aid Map Report](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/publications/2026-pacific-aid-map-report) around 28 July, confirming Australia as by far the [dominant Pacific aid partner](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/publications/2026-pacific-aid-map-report/australia-remains-the-dominant-pacific-aid-partner). The report, which tracks US$60 billion across roughly 50,000 projects from 2008–2024, found Australia disbursed US$1.5 billion in the Pacific last year, more than three times the next-largest donor (New Zealand) and accounting for 37 percent of all development finance in the region. A companion piece notes [China now favours frequent, smaller grants](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/publications/2026-pacific-aid-map-report/china-now-favours-frequent-small-grants-as-big-project-lending-subsides) over big-ticket lending, a shift with implications for how Canberra positions its own aid offer.

That positioning is playing out in real time. Reporting out of Port Moresby and Suva this week says [Australia is helping Pacific Island nations "dodge worst of aid cuts"](https://pina.com.fj/2026/07/29/australia-helps-pacific-islands-dodge-worst-of-aid-cuts/) as US and other donor retrenchment squeezes regional budgets, even as a senior Australian envoy [warned that Western democracies are losing ground](https://pina.com.fj/2026/07/21/envoy-warns-pacific-strategic-contest-is-shifting-as-png-says-it-will-close-taiwan-office/) in the broader Pacific strategic contest, coinciding with Papua New Guinea's move to close its Taiwan representative office. The combination of a record Australian aid footprint and intensifying geopolitical competition is likely to shape how DFAT and Australian NGOs pitch further Pacific humanitarian and resilience funding in the months ahead.

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

Cyclone activity has been unusually busy across the Eastern and Western Pacific this week. PDC's DisasterAWARE tracker showed [Tropical Cyclone 06E (Fausto)](https://www.pdc.org/weather-wall-updates/tropical-cyclone-06e-fausto-tropical-cyclone-07e-genevieve-tropical-cyclone-11w-noul-pacific/) reaching sustained winds near 105 mph before weakening to post-tropical status, while Tropical Cyclone 07E (Genevieve) and Tropical Cyclone 12W (Dolphin) were both active as of 29 July. None of the current systems are yet flagged as high humanitarian impact by GDACS, but the cluster of concurrent storms is keeping Pacific and Philippine disaster agencies on alert heading into August.

On the diplomatic-humanitarian front, the East Asia Summit and ASEAN Regional Forum, held in Manila from 21–23 July, saw the [East Asia Summit express concern over the conflict and humanitarian situation in Myanmar](https://eng.mizzima.com/2026/07/26/36733), while welcoming continued ASEAN, UN and private-sector support for the AHA Centre's Myanmar assistance work. The same gathering doubled as a broader security forum, with [top diplomats discussing the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz blockade](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/07/southeast-asia-hosts-security-gathering-amid-middle-east-turmoil) alongside disaster response cooperation, underscoring how Middle East instability is now a standing agenda item even at Asia-Pacific regional forums.

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

Gaza and the West Bank remain the most acute crisis on the board. OCHA's [23 July Humanitarian Situation Report](https://www.unocha.org/publications/report/occupied-palestinian-territory/opt-humanitarian-situation-report-23-july-2026) recorded more than 1,200 settler-related incidents across the West Bank so far in 2026 — about six a day — with 18 Palestinians killed by 20 July, already surpassing all of 2025's toll, and over 3,200 people displaced by settler attacks and demolitions. Inside Gaza, [Israel struck the territory after issuing new displacement orders, killing two Palestinians](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/24/israel-bombs-gaza-after-displacement-orders-kills-two-palestinians) on 24 July, as humanitarian agencies continue to report severe constraints on access near the "Yellow Line" in northern Rafah and a documented rise in miscarriages linked to the deteriorating health situation.

Funding pressure is compounding the access problem. WFP says it urgently [needs US$426 million between July and December](https://www.wfp.org/emergencies/palestine-emergency) to keep supporting more than two million people in Gaza and the West Bank, while roughly two-thirds of Gaza's population remains at crisis-level food insecurity. Elsewhere in Africa, WFP [warned that food assistance for Congolese refugees in Burundi risks ending entirely](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2026/07/mil-260724-wfp01.htm) without an urgent US$35 million injection, with distributions set to stop from October if funding does not arrive. Against this backdrop, the [United States announced more than US$200 million in humanitarian assistance to the ICRC and IFRC](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/07/united-states-announces-more-than-200-million-in-humanitarian-assistance-to-the-icrc-and-ifrc) around 23 July, split roughly US$133 million to ICRC's 2026 Global Appeal and US$70 million to IFRC — a rare bright spot in an otherwise strained funding picture.

Cholera remains a live threat on the continent. Outbreaks first confirmed in June continue to spread in both [Sudan, where the case fatality rate has climbed to 13.7 percent](https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/what-sudans-cholera-outbreak-reveals-about-its-fragile-health-system), and the [Central African Republic, where UNICEF reports 435 suspected cases and 36 deaths](https://reliefweb.int/report/central-african-republic/unicef-central-african-republic-humanitarian-flash-update-no-1-cholera-outbreak-response-6-july-2026) along the Ubangi River, with children under 10 making up 44 percent of cases.

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

FAO and WFP's first-ever [Joint Anticipatory Action Appeal for El Niño](https://www.wfp.org/publications/el-nino-fao-wfp-joint-anticipatory-action-appeal-june-2026-march-2027) is seeking US$202 million to protect 8.8 million people across 22 high-risk countries in Africa, Asia-Pacific and Latin America from extreme weather through March 2027. The appeal sits alongside WFP's broader warning that it is [prioritising feeding just 110 million of the world's hungriest people in 2026](https://www.wfp.org/news/wfp-prioritize-feeding-110-million-hungriest-2026-global-hunger-deepens-amidst-uncertain) as global hunger deepens and donor funding remains uncertain.

In Ukraine, UNHCR published a July 2026 [policy brief on the state strategy for internal displacement](https://reliefweb.int/report/ukraine/unhcr-ukraine-policy-brief-state-strategy-internal-displacement-july-2026), alongside a revised Humanitarian Action for Children plan for the Ukraine refugee response. Globally, UNHCR and partner analyses continue to put forced displacement above 122 million people, with Sudan (2.8 million refugees, 9.1 million internally displaced), Syria (15.6 million in need) and DRC (over 1.17 million refugees) among the largest single-country crises.

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

GDACS tracked a cluster of tropical cyclones through the week: [Tropical Cyclone Bertha](https://www.gdacs.org/report.aspx?eventtype=TC&eventid=1001290) affected the United States at green (low) alert from 19–22 July, while Tropical Cyclone Fausto intensified to 105 mph winds before weakening, and Tropical Cyclones Genevieve and Dolphin remained active in the Pacific and Western Pacific basins as of 29 July. A tsunami message was also issued via the Pacific Tsunami Warning Center on 17 July for the Pacific basin, though no major impact was reported. None of this week's systems have been assessed by GDACS as high humanitarian impact, but the concurrent activity warrants continued monitoring into August.
