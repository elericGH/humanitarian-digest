# ⚡ Daily Humanitarian Update — Thursday, 13 August 2026
*Produced Thursday, 13 August 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Foreign Minister Penny Wong tabled the new Australia-Fiji "Ocean of Peace Alliance" and the Vuvale Union Treaty in Parliament on 12 August, describing them as a multi-generational deepening of security, economic and humanitarian cooperation with Fiji, backed by more than A$1 billion in Australian investment over 10 years. Wong said the Alliance, Australia's fourth and Fiji's first, will strengthen joint disaster response planning and interoperability alongside defence cooperation. [PACNEWS/Islands Business](https://islandsbusiness.com/pacnews/pacnews-one-12-august-2026/)

Pacific Foreign Ministers, including Australia, endorsed a draft Regional Peace and Security Action Plan 2026–2030 on 12 August for consideration by Forum Leaders at the 55th Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting in Palau. The plan explicitly names humanitarian emergencies alongside transnational crime and climate-linked instability as priority regional risks requiring resourced, Forum-led response mechanisms. [PINA](https://pina.com.fj/2026/08/12/pacific-foreign-ministers-back-regional-security-plan-as-threats-grow-more-complex/)

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

Papua New Guinea has locked in access to up to K300 million (approx. A$126 million) in World Bank disaster financing to respond to worsening El Niño drought conditions, Treasurer Ian Ling-Stuckey confirmed after meeting Bank officials in Singapore on 7 August. The package combines a US$50 million Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option built into PNG's budget support loan with up to K80 million drawn from the Rural Service Delivery Project's Contingent Emergency Response Component. [The National, PNG](https://www.thenational.com.pg/world-bank-may-lend-k300mil-for-el-nino-disaster-relief/)

Flooding returned to parts of Metro Manila on 12 August after continuous southwest monsoon rain, even after some streets had briefly cleared. The combined effects of the monsoon and Tropical Cyclones Kujira and Dolphin have now affected close to 586,000 people across nine regions and 33 provinces, with roughly 32,800 people displaced and at least 12 dead and 8 missing. [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2026/08/12/2548823/list-flooded-metro-manila-areas-aug-12)

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

IOM is appealing for US$19 million to scale up its emergency response in Lebanon, where the number of people forced to flee their homes has continued to surge; separately, OCHA reporting shows 240,000 displaced people assisted with food and cash in Lebanon to date, alongside 24,500 displaced people who received date bars at Syria border crossings. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

In Mali, more than 415,000 people remain internally displaced — mostly women and children — driven by insecurity, climate shocks and health crises, according to the latest humanitarian situation update circulating on ReliefWeb. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

The South Sudan Humanitarian Country Team and UNMISS issued a joint call for peace and civilian protection in Akobo, with Troika envoys urging President Kiir to act, amid continuing common pipeline support reaching thousands with shelter and onward transportation assistance in South Sudan. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

Heavy rain has caused flooding and casualties in West Java Province, Indonesia, with at least three people injured according to the ASEAN Disaster Information Network (ADINet), as authorities continue damage assessments. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

In Latin America, an estimated 16,000 people have been affected by rains and flooding across Peru, while 2–3 million people are estimated to need food assistance in Honduras and Guatemala between June and August, and roughly 44,000 people are affected by ongoing rain and flooding in Ecuador and neighbouring countries. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

The Philippines remains the most significant active sudden-onset event: ongoing southwest monsoon rain combined with the aftermath of Tropical Cyclones Kujira and Dolphin continues to cause flooding in Metro Manila and provincial areas, with casualties, missing persons and tens of thousands displaced (see Tier 1 above). [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2026/08/12/2548823/list-flooded-metro-manila-areas-aug-12)

Flash flooding is also active in West Java, Indonesia, following heavy rain, with early injury and damage reports still being confirmed. [ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/updates)

A GDACS alert search run at time of writing returned no new Red or Orange sudden-onset events beyond the systems above; monitoring continues.
