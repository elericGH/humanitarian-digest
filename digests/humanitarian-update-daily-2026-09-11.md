*Produced Friday, 11 September 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Australia's response to the catastrophic 26 August Nepal–Tibet border floods remains the dominant Australian-interest story this week. DFAT's crisis hub confirms a Family Support Hub is running in Kathmandu with Services Australia, and that additional consular, AFP Disaster Victim Identification, National Emergency Management Agency and Defence personnel (including drone operators) remain deployed to support the response ([DFAT Crisis Hub](https://www.dfat.gov.au/crisis-hub/flooding-nepal-and-china)). The Australian Government has also contributed AUD 2 million to the World Food Programme's emergency food and logistics operation for flood-affected communities in Rasuwa and downstream districts ([WFP](https://www.wfp.org/news/government-australia-supports-wfps-emergency-response-flood-affected-communities-nepal)).

Emergency Action Alliance, the coalition of 15 Australian aid charities (including ADRA Australia), continues to run its Nepal Flood Disaster Appeal, with member agencies distributing shelter, food and basic supplies ([Emergency Action Alliance](https://emergencyaction.org.au/)).

No new dated announcements from ACFID were identified in the past 24 hours; its most recent public material relates to the 2026 Federal Budget analysis and the upcoming ACFID National Conference (12–14 October, Canberra) ([ACFID](https://acfid.asn.au/)).

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

The 2026 Nepal floods disaster remains the largest active Asia-Pacific humanitarian response, with Nepalese authorities reporting 1,369 deaths and 5,132 people still missing, and Chinese authorities reporting 43 deaths and 519 missing in the Tibet Autonomous Region ([DFAT Crisis Hub](https://www.dfat.gov.au/crisis-hub/flooding-nepal-and-china)). Scientists continue to flag a second glacial lake near the flood zone as a further hazard risk ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/28/world/live-news/nepal-china-flood)).

Regional Pacific reporting through Islands Business/PACNEWS this week has focused on disaster-preparedness policy work rather than an active new emergency, including Papua New Guinea's Department of Education rollout of its Resilient School Infrastructure Policy for schools facing flood, earthquake, cyclone and landslide risk ([Islands Business PACNEWS](https://islandsbusiness.com/pacnews/pacnews-three-wednesday-9-september-2026/)).

No new items from the Pacific Disaster Center, East Asia Forum, The Diplomat or Indo-Pacific Defense Forum were identified in the past 24 hours.

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

Fighting that escalated rapidly on 3 September along Yemen's west coast and in parts of Taiz and Al-Hodeidah governorates has displaced roughly 18,500 people; IOM says the figure continues to rise as families arrive in host communities with no shelter or food, and that clashes and telecoms disruption are hampering efforts to register the newly displaced ([IOM](https://www.iom.int/news/18500-displaced-72-hours-fighting-flares-across-yemen-west-coast)).

The Ebola outbreak caused by the Bundibugyo virus in the Democratic Republic of Congo continues to expand rapidly: as of 6–7 September, DRC had recorded 6,686 confirmed cases and 3,226 deaths, now the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, with no licensed vaccine available for this strain ([WHO Disease Outbreak News](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON603)).

WFP has warned that funding shortfalls have forced it to halve cash and food assistance to roughly 200,000 people in the occupied West Bank, where almost a million people (over a quarter of the population) are now food insecure, up from 11% two years ago; WFP says it needs US$386 million to avoid further cuts across Gaza and the West Bank over the next six months ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168237)).

Sudan's civil war continues to drive one of the world's largest displacement crises, with over 11.8 million people displaced (7.4 million internally, 4.2 million in neighbouring countries) as the UN Security Council worked this month to renew the Sudan sanctions regime ahead of its 12 September expiry ([Security Council Report](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/monthly-forecast/2026-09/sudan-41.php)).
