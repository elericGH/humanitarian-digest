## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

The Nepal-Tibet flood disaster remains the dominant Australian-interest story. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, speaking from Palau where he is attending a regional summit, confirmed that the number of Australians unaccounted for in the flood zone has fallen from 43 to 38 after five more were confirmed safe overnight, as the confirmed death toll in Nepal climbed past 1,100 ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/International/nepal-tibet-flood-death-toll-tops-1100-tens/story?id=136136947)).

Australia's overall humanitarian response to the disaster now totals $8 million, including a $3 million second tranche announced by Foreign Minister Penny Wong: $2 million for emergency supplies (hygiene kits, solar lighting) delivered via the Australian Defence Force, and a further $1 million to the Emergency Action Alliance (EAA) appeal, bringing Australia's EAA contribution to $2 million. Around 12 Australian crisis-response, consular and AFP personnel remain in Kathmandu ([Foreign Minister media release](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/additional-humanitarian-support-nepal)).

The Emergency Action Alliance's 15-member coalition (including ADRA) continues to run its Nepal Flood Disaster Appeal, providing emergency shelter, clean water, food and medical assistance through member charities and local partners, with the appeal page most recently updated on 31 August ([EAA Nepal Flood Appeal](https://emergencyaction.org.au/nepal-flood-appeal/)).

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

The World Food Programme confirmed it is on the ground in Nepal delivering emergency food relief following floods that have killed more than 1,100 people and left thousands missing ([WFP USA](https://wfpusa.org/news/wfp-halves-food-assistance-west-bank/)).

Indonesia and the United States kicked off annual multinational military exercises involving more than 6,000 personnel from 14 nations, with this year's drills focused mostly on humanitarian assistance and disaster relief (HADR) cooperation across the Asia-Pacific ([The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/09/indonesia-united-states-kick-off-annual-multinational-military-exercises/)).

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

The UN World Food Programme announced it is halving the number of people it can support with food assistance in the West Bank, from 400,000 to 200,000, due to critical funding shortfalls, even as food needs have more than doubled since 2023. WFP says it needs an additional US$386 million over the next six months to serve roughly two million food-insecure people across Gaza and the West Bank ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2026/09/01/funding-cuts-force-un-s-wfp-to-halve-west-bank-aid-recipients), [WFP USA](https://wfpusa.org/news/wfp-halves-food-assistance-west-bank/)).

OCHA reporting on Sudan notes continued displacement pressure, with nearly 22,000 people fleeing recent fighting in Blue Nile State, against a backdrop of more than 30 million people in need nationally and 12 million displaced ([OCHA Sudan](https://www.unocha.org/sudan)).

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

The Nepal-Tibet flood emergency remains the region's active sudden-onset hazard, with a Copernicus Emergency Mapping Service activation in place since 31 August and search-and-rescue operations continuing for thousands still missing ([GDACS](https://www.gdacs.org/Alerts/)). Tropical Storm Risk recorded six active tropical cyclone systems globally as of 2 September, none currently posing a direct threat to Australia or the Pacific Islands region based on available tracking ([Tropical Storm Risk](https://www.tropicalstormrisk.com/)).
