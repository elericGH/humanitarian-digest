# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 22 September 2026 – 29 September 2026
*Produced Tuesday, 29 September 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Australia's signature humanitarian diplomacy initiative reached a milestone at the UN General Assembly this week. The Declaration for the Protection of Humanitarian Personnel, which Australia led alongside Brazil, Colombia, Indonesia, Japan, Jordan, Sierra Leone, Switzerland and the UK, now has 130 signatory countries, more than two-thirds of UN member states, one year after launch. Foreign Minister Penny Wong announced that Australia is partnering with the UN Department of Safety and Security, the ICRC and others on security analysis and training for aid workers, better evidence collection to support accountability, and legal and financial help for victims and their families ([Minister for Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/declaration-protection-humanitarian-personnel-achieves-historic-support)). ICRC President Mirjana Spoljaric addressed the inaugural meeting of signatories on 23 September, warning that humanitarian workers are being killed in record numbers ([ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/statement/icrc-president-safety-humanitarian-personnel-inseparable-safety-all-civilians)).

On 24 September, Prime Minister Albanese and President Zelenskyy signed the Australia–Ukraine Bilateral Security Agreement. The accompanying A$60 million package includes A$45 million in ADF equipment, A$10 million for Ukraine's Energy Support Fund and A$5 million in humanitarian assistance, taking Australia's total support to almost A$1.9 billion. Australia also joined the UK and Norway-led Maritime Capability Coalition and added further sanctions on Russia ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/agreement-long-term-support-and-cooperation-between-australia-and-ukraine); [Defence Connect](https://www.defenceconnect.com.au/land/18908-albanese-zelenskyy-sign-bilateral-security-agreement-amid-60m-in-support-to-ukraine)).

For the sector at home, the ACFID National Conference 2026 is set for 12–14 October at the National Convention Centre Canberra ([ACFID](https://acfid.asn.au/members/conference-2026/)). No new Emergency Action Alliance appeal launched during the week ([Emergency Action Alliance](https://emergencyaction.org.au/current-appeals)).

The trend line is that Canberra is putting its money into protection and accountability norms, which cost relatively little, while large new bilateral humanitarian funding announcements were absent this week. That matters for agencies facing the global funding squeeze described in Tier 2.

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

**Mainland Southeast Asia floods.** A strong weather system has caused severe flooding across Thailand and southern Myanmar. The AHA Centre reports that since 16 September, flooding has affected 41 Thai provinces and Bangkok: 582,500 households (1.81 million people), with eight deaths. As of 28 September it was still ongoing in 27 provinces and Bangkok ([AHA Centre](https://ahacentre.org/weekly-disaster-update/weekly-disaster-update-21-27-september-2026/)). More than 80,000 people in Bangkok were reported unable to leave their homes ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/09/28/383278/world/deadly-thailand-floods-kill-8-bangkok-inundated-as-80000-stranded-5-8-lakh-homes-destroyed-markets-floating/)). ACT Alliance has issued an alert for the Thailand floods ([ACT Alliance](https://actalliance.org/alerts/act-alert-thailand-floods-emergency-september-2026/)). Across the border, Tanintharyi Region in Myanmar recorded its highest rainfall in 80 years. Resistance sources reported at least 13 dead and 30 missing in Dawei District, and many villages could only be reached on foot ([The Irrawaddy](https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/at-least-13-killed-in-floods-in-resistance-held-dawei.html); [The Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/09/28/world/asia-oceania/myanmar-rescuers-fear-10-dead-in-cut-off-flood-zone/2433816)).

**Wider ASEAN picture.** The AHA Centre logged 23 disasters across Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand and Viet Nam in Week 39. It also noted that ENSO monitoring shows "El Niño Conditions", with strong El Niño forecast until at least the end of 2026. That points to drier conditions across much of the Maritime Continent in the months ahead, a drought and fire risk for Indonesia and Timor-Leste ([AHA Centre](https://ahacentre.org/weekly-disaster-update/weekly-disaster-update-21-27-september-2026/)).

**Pacific leaders at UNGA.** At the UN high-level plenary on sea-level rise, PNG Prime Minister James Marape and Solomon Islands leader Matthew Wale urged industrialised nations to stop the "empty promises" and cut emissions ([Asia Pacific Report](https://asiapacificreport.nz/2026/09/26/stop-empty-promises-papua-new-guinea-solomon-islands-leaders-demand-climate-action/)). The Pacific Islands News roundup for 17–23 September also covered a growing HIV crisis that civil society says was left off the regional health agenda, and continuing gaps in social protection: coverage is below 30% in Solomon Islands and PNG ([Pacific Islands Report](https://pireport.org/2026/09/24/pacific-islands-news-roundup-september-17-september-23-2026/)).

**Rohingya.** Nine years on, the UN described the Rohingya situation as one of the world's most protracted refugee crises and called for a future "beyond survival" ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168417)).

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

**Sudan.** The war, now in its fourth year, is still pushing people across borders. More than 55,000 Sudanese refugees have reached eastern Chad so far this year, and almost 1 million since April 2023, while UNHCR warns funding is falling short ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/more-than-55000-sudanese-refugees-flee-to-chad-as-un-calls-for-funding)). Violence against civilians continued. A monitoring group reported that an army drone strike on 25 September killed at least nine civilians and wounded 24 in Al-Mazrub, North Kordofan ([Sudan Tribune](https://sudantribune.com/article/319263)). This follows mid-month UN warnings that Sudan's humanitarian system "could collapse" without new funding, with IOM relief stocks projected to run out by the end of September ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168331)).

**DR Congo Ebola (Bundibugyo).** More than 130 days in, WHO reported that deaths have passed 3,700. Cases fell by about 26% in Ituri and 15% in Haut-Uélé over 21 days but rose 73% in North Kivu. Around one in three new cases is identified only after death in the community, and children under five have a case fatality rate above 60% ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168407)). By 25 September the outbreak had spread to two new health zones, Bulu (Sud-Ubangi) and Dungu (Haut-Uélé) ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/dr-congos-ebola-outbreak-spreads-to-two-new-health-zones-who-says)). WHO says staffing, not beds, is now the biggest gap.

**Occupied Palestinian Territory.** OCHA's 25 September report records 147 Palestinians, including 73 children, displaced by demolitions in Area C and East Jerusalem in a single reporting period, more than three times the 2026 weekly average. Communities near the E1 settlement area are particularly at risk ([OCHA oPt](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-25-september-2026)). The ICRC warned that it still has no access to Palestinian detainees in Israeli prisons, and that drones and new warfare technologies are undermining humanitarian access more broadly ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/25/icrc-warns-of-shrinking-access-to-war-zones-as-palestinian-detainees-remain-out-of-reach/)).

**Yemen.** UNHCR warned on 22 September that displacement is rising as fighting worsens ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168390)).

**Trend:** Across Sudan, Gaza and the West Bank, the week's reporting keeps coming back to the same point: needs are growing while agencies cut caseloads because of funding shortfalls.

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

**Global displacement.** On 25 September, UN High Commissioner for Refugees Barham Salih gave his first Security Council briefing. He warned that "displacement is rapidly outpacing solutions", with more than 117 million people forcibly displaced and Sudan the largest crisis at around 12 million ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168438); [GlobalSecurity.org / UN](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2026/09/mil-260925-unsc01.htm)). UN figures cited at the meeting include 41.6 million refugees and 68.7 million internally displaced people, with 68% of refugees hosted by low- and middle-income countries.

**EU funding.** On 26 September, European Commission President von der Leyen announced more than €710 million in humanitarian and crisis funding. It includes €380 million for migration-related measures in sub-Saharan Africa, €97 million for humanitarian needs in sub-Saharan Africa (including the DRC Ebola response), €103 million for the Palestinian territories and Lebanon, and €52 million for Ukraine ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/27/eu-unlocks-humanitarian-aid-for-africa-and-other-crisis-hit-regions)).

**Ukraine.** Australia's new A$60 million package (see Australian Priority Alerts) was one of several pledges made at UNGA week ([Prime Minister of Australia](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/agreement-long-term-support-and-cooperation-between-australia-and-ukraine)).

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

**Super Typhoon Surigae ("Queenie").** Surigae entered the Philippine Area of Responsibility on 24 September as a tropical storm ([Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/weather/2026/09/24/2558609/tropical-storm-surigae-enters-par-now-called-queenie)). It reached super typhoon strength late on 27 September and tracked towards Okinawa and Kyushu, then left the PAR by 28 September ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/25/typhoon-surigae-moves-closer-to-japans-okinawa-kyushu); [RainViewer](https://www.rainviewer.com/hurricanes/surigae-2026.html)). The AHA Centre is also monitoring Invest 92W over Myanmar ([AHA Centre](https://ahacentre.org/weekly-disaster-update/weekly-disaster-update-21-27-september-2026/)).

**Hurricane Nolo, Hawaii.** Nolo stalled near the Big Island at Category 4, bringing rainfall of up to 20 inches and flash-flood and mudslide risk to ground already saturated by Hurricane Lala in August ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/weather/2026/09/26/hurricane-nolo-stalls-near-hawaiis-big-island-serious-flooding-likely/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/25/nx-s1-5981082/hurricane-nolo-hawaii)). Three Pacific hurricanes have reached Category 5 this season, tying a record, in an El Niño-fuelled year ([US News / AP](https://www.usnews.com/news/news/articles/2026-09-24/its-been-an-intense-hurricane-season-in-the-pacific-and-theres-still-a-long-way-to-go)).

**M6.6 earthquake, New Caledonia.** On 26 September a M6.6 earthquake struck about 68 km east-northeast of Tadine in the Loyalty Islands. There were no reports of major damage in the alert, but aftershocks and unusual currents were flagged ([U.S. Embassy Suva](https://fj.usembassy.gov/natural-disaster-alert-6-6-earthquake-near-new-caledonia-in-the-pacific-ocean-september-26-2026/)).

**Thailand and Myanmar floods.** See Tier 1. Indonesia's BMKG also recorded three M>5.0 earthquakes, and seven volcanoes in Indonesia and the Philippines showed activity ([AHA Centre](https://ahacentre.org/weekly-disaster-update/weekly-disaster-update-21-27-september-2026/)).
