# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 2 October 2026 – 9 October 2026
*Produced Friday, 9 October 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

**Papua New Guinea's El Niño drought declared a humanitarian emergency.** PNG's Minister for Health and National Disaster Services, Billy Joseph, told a National Disaster Centre meeting in Port Moresby that the drought "is no longer just a prolonged dry season", with 20 of PNG's 22 provincial-level divisions in extreme or severe drought and provincial reports estimating around 5.5 million people affected, exposed or at high risk. Health authorities report rising risks of diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid, malaria, skin infections and malnutrition, and the NDC has asked leaders in three provinces, including the National Capital District, to take critical water-saving measures ([Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261008/35072bdbffa0444284e2f0e66410901a/c.html), [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-08/news-1R4di1btjhK/p.html)). As Australia's closest neighbour and largest bilateral aid partner, this is the most significant Australian-interest development of the week. The Australian Bureau of Meteorology's 29 September update said indices are consistent with a strong El Niño, with further warming forecast through spring. No new DFAT funding announcement for PNG was found during the week; watch for one.

**UN agencies frame El Niño as a Pacific-wide risk.** UN News reported that El Niño is forecast to peak between October and December, with about 1.2 million people in PNG estimated to need direct food relief alongside water shortages, crop losses and school disruption ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168537)). UNICEF separately warned that 347 million children in nearly 100 countries face unusually wet or dry conditions, including 190 million in Asia and the Pacific, with Fiji, PNG and the Philippines named among countries at risk of agricultural drought. UNICEF noted that under 1% of humanitarian funding goes to anticipatory action ([Vatican News / UNICEF](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/world/news/2026-10/unicef-el-nino-extreme-weather-events-children-affected-appeal.html)).

**Sector news: ACFID annual report and conference.** ACFID has published its Annual Report 2025-26 and its national conference, "Mobilising the Collective: Reshaping Systems for a Just Future", runs 12–14 October. ACFID's active consolidated appeals remain Nepal and Tibet Floods, the Venezuela Earthquake, the DRC Ebola outbreak and the Middle East Humanitarian Crisis ([ACFID](https://acfid.asn.au/nepal-and-tibet-floods-appeal/)). The Emergency Action Alliance's 15 member agencies continue their Nepal Flood Disaster Appeal ([Emergency Action Alliance](https://emergencyaction.org.au/)).

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

**El Niño is the defining story for the Pacific this quarter.** Beyond the PNG emergency, Pacific leaders used pre-COP31 meetings this week to push the Pacific Resilience Facility (PRF), which would fund community-level seawalls, water security and housing protection before disasters hit. Fiji announced US$10 million for the PRF, with further pledges from Denmark (US$2.3 million) and Solomon Islands (US$1.1 million). Fiji's Foreign Minister Sakiasi Ditoka argued water-storage tanks must be deployed "at scale before the rains stop" ([Islands Business PACNEWS, 7 Oct](https://islandsbusiness.com/pacnews/pacnews-two-07-october-2026/), [PACNEWS, 8 Oct](https://islandsbusiness.com/pacnews/pacnews-three-08-october-2026)). PNG has also committed 25 million kina (about US$5.6 million) to Tuvalu's land reclamation ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168537)). The trend: Pacific governments are building their own anticipatory financing while the drought sharpens.

**Thailand's floods remain severe.** Floods since 16 September have killed 31 people and affected about 4.14 million, with roughly 3.5 million people in 26 provinces and Bangkok still affected in early October. Around 700,000 people in Bangkok were hit after about 320 mm of rain in a few days. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul ordered faster drainage and relief as more rain and high tides were forecast ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40071861), [CDP](https://disasterphilanthropy.org/blog/what-were-watching-weekly-disaster-update-october-5-2026/)).

**Myanmar: floods on top of conflict.** Flash floods and landslides in Tanintharyi Region killed at least 41 people, with 20 missing. Roads and communications were cut and rescues were hampered by a lack of heavy machinery, compounding an already dire humanitarian emergency ([CDP](https://disasterphilanthropy.org/blog/what-were-watching-weekly-disaster-update-october-5-2026/)).

**Rohingya response under financial strain.** The New Humanitarian's 2 October Cheat Sheet flagged Rohingya repatriation as a live issue ([The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/)). Context: nearly 1.2 million Rohingya remain in Bangladesh, 95% of families depend on aid, and the 2026 joint response plan seeks US$710.5 million, 26% less than last year ([UN News, Sept](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168417)).

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

**Ethiopia: Tigray war resumes amid looming famine.** Fighting between the TPLF-aligned "Alliance for Survival" and federal forces resumed on 23 September. Federal forces entered Mekelle without a fight on 4 October after TPLF forces withdrew. Communications were restored after more than a week ([Africanews/AFP](https://www.africanews.com/2026/10/05/ethiopias-federal-forces-retake-tigrayan-capital-mekelle/)). The New Humanitarian reports a roughly 24-hour power vacuum in which armed groups looted the Tigray Disaster Risk Management Commission and the NGO OSSHD (around 60 computers, about US$1 million in non-food items and most vehicles), halting services to about 7,000 children affected by HIV. A drone strike also hit a food aid convoy in the northwest. Before the fighting, 3.9 million people (over half of Tigray's population) already needed emergency food aid after El Niño-driven crop failure on over 252,500 hectares. About 3.2 million people in need are not covered by current food distributions. The 460 km Mekelle–Semera corridor was functional as of 4 October, and a humanitarian pause is being urged ([The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2026/10/08/tigray-faces-double-catastrophe-war-and-worsening-hunger)). Regional risk is rising: Egypt, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan's leaders met in El-Alamein on 4 October.

**Sudan: Kordofan escalation and drone war on civilians.** OCHA says it is deeply alarmed by sharp escalation across Kordofan, including attacks on kindergartens and hospitals. A strike on a major power station in Blue Nile State caused blackouts in Damazin, disrupting water and health services ([OCHA Sudan](https://www.unocha.org/sudan)). El Obeid, whose population has roughly doubled from displacement, faces siege-like conditions with water shortages, cholera and reports of sexual violence. A 1 October drone strike on University of Kordofan student housing killed five and wounded 47 ([Stimson Center, 7 Oct](https://stimson.org/2026/north-africa-regional-outlook-october-7-2026), [Security Council Report](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/monthly-forecast/2026-10/sudan-42.php)).

**Gaza: funding cuts hit food as winter approaches.** OCHA's 2 October situation report (published 5 October) warns that daily hot meal production and monthly household food parcels will shrink significantly from October because of funding shortfalls. About 279,000 people are in 400 flood-prone displacement sites, and nearly 44,000 live in over 2,300 unsafe buildings, together more than 13% of the population. Desalination output in the south has fallen to its lowest levels for want of spare parts, threatening water for about 500,000 people, and 74% of the population relies on trucked water. MoH reports 1,431 deaths since the October 2025 ceasefire announcement. In the West Bank, 73 structures were demolished or sealed in a week, displacing 93 people, and demolitions continue in Masafer Yatta ([OCHA oPt](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-2-october-2026)).

**Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz.** Iranian media reported "massive explosions" in the southern Strait of Hormuz on 9 October, possibly tankers hitting mines on unauthorised routes. US President Trump said talks with Iran were "productive" ([Al Jazeera live](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/10/9/iran-war-live-iranian-media-reports-massive-explosions-in-hormuz-strait)). The ongoing disruption is forcing WFP and NGOs to reroute aid, adding weeks and costs. WFP has reported food price rises of about 20% in Somalia and Myanmar ([Think Global Health](https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/how-the-iran-war-is-straining-humanitarian-aid-in-three-charts)).

**DRC Ebola spreads to Kenya.** Kenya confirmed its first Ebola case on 6 October: a Kenyan national who travelled from the DRC via Kampala died in Nairobi. Twenty-eight contacts were identified and passengers on his flight are being traced. WHO advised against travel and trade restrictions. The DRC outbreak (Bundibugyo strain) has passed 8,300 confirmed cases and 4,000 deaths, the worst in the country's history ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/6/kenya-confirms-first-ebola-case-after-patient-from-dr-congo-dies-in-nairobi)). Daily case rates appear to have peaked in August at about 120 per day, falling to about 80 by mid-September ([CDP](https://disasterphilanthropy.org/blog/what-were-watching-weekly-disaster-update-october-5-2026/)).

**Nigeria: a deepening complex emergency.** WFP estimates at least 35 million people face severe food insecurity, UNICEF projects three million children needing treatment for severe wasting in 2026, and the country hosts over 3.6 million IDPs. El Niño could intensify drought and flooding through 2027 ([CDP](https://disasterphilanthropy.org/blog/what-were-watching-weekly-disaster-update-october-5-2026/)).

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

**Spain: deadly Catalonia flash floods.** At least two people died and one is missing after torrential rain in Catalonia. More than 90 rescues were carried out, and flights, trains and events were cancelled ([CDP](https://disasterphilanthropy.org/blog/what-were-watching-weekly-disaster-update-october-5-2026/)).

**United States: Hurricane Isaias.** Tropical Cyclone ISAIAS-26 struck the US on 6–7 October as a Category 2 storm (176 km/h), exposing about 2.1 million people to Category 1+ winds. GDACS rated humanitarian impact as low (Green alert) ([GDACS](https://www.gdacs.org/report.aspx?eventtype=TC&eventid=1001334)).

**Aid sector.** The New Humanitarian's "Inklings" newsletter (5 October) questioned the German government over WFP's relationship with Palantir, part of a wider debate on data protection in humanitarian tech ([The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/)).
