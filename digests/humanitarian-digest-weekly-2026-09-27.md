# 🌍 Weekly Humanitarian Digest — 20 September 2026 – 27 September 2026
*Produced Sunday, 27 September 2026 | Australian Interest Priority Edition*

---

## 🇦🇺 Australian Priority Alerts

Australia led two significant multilateral moves on humanitarian protection this week. On 21 September, Foreign Minister Penny Wong joined the MIKTA grouping (Mexico, Indonesia, the Republic of Korea, Türkiye and Australia) in a [Joint Statement on the Protection of Humanitarian Personnel and Humanitarian Action from Misinformation, Disinformation and Harmful Narratives](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/joint-statement-protection-humanitarian-personnel-and-humanitarian-action-misinformation-disinformation-and-harmful-narratives), condemning the coordinated spread of false and harmful narratives that incite violence against aid workers or obstruct humanitarian access. The statement reaffirms UN Security Council Resolution 2730 and links directly to Australia's own [Declaration for the Protection of Humanitarian Personnel](https://www.foreignminister.gov.au/minister/penny-wong/media-release/declaration-protection-humanitarian-personnel-achieves-historic-support), which this week secured landmark support from more than two-thirds of UN member states at an inaugural signatories' meeting on the sidelines of UNGA — ICRC president Mirjana Spoljaric told the 23 September gathering that humanitarian workers are "being killed in record numbers."

Separately, the government's Ukraine engagement deepened: the Prime Minister and Ukraine's President signed the Australia-Ukraine Bilateral Security Agreement this week, underpinning a $60 million package that includes military equipment, support for Ukraine's Energy Support Fund, and humanitarian assistance ([Australian Institute of International Affairs, 25 September](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/25-september-2026-this-week-in-australian-foreign-affairs/)).

On the funding side, [ACFID's analysis of the Federal Budget](https://acfid.asn.au/federal-budget-holds-for-aid/) continues to frame the week's diplomacy against a harder truth: Australian aid remains at just 0.63 percent of the Budget — an all-time low in generosity — even as the government has increased Indo-Pacific-specific investment and protected the aid program from the cuts seen elsewhere globally. The 15-member [Emergency Action Alliance](https://emergencyaction.org.au/), which includes ADRA, continues coordinated fundraising operations for current international emergencies.

## 🌏 Tier 1 — Pacific & Asia-Pacific

A magnitude 6.6–7 earthquake struck roughly 68km east-northeast of Tadine in New Caledonia's Loyalty Islands at 8:23am local time on 26 September ([U.S. Embassy Fiji alert](https://fj.usembassy.gov/natural-disaster-alert-6-6-earthquake-near-new-caledonia-in-the-pacific-ocean-september-26-2026/); [USGS via US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-09-25/earthquake-of-magnitude-7-strikes-tadine-new-caledonia-region-usgs-reports)). No tsunami threat was declared for Hawaii, Guam, the Marianas or American Samoa, and no casualties or major damage have been reported, though authorities warned of possible damaging aftershocks and unusual nearshore currents.

Health and climate pressures are compounding across the Pacific. Nearly half of Palau's essential medicines are out of stock as global supply chains strain ([Island Times](https://islandtimes.org/nearly-half-of-palaus-essential-medicines-out-of-stock-as-global-supply-chain-strains-bite/)), while El Niño is putting growing pressure on rural farmers and water supplies in Vanuatu ([Daily Post](https://www.dailypost.vu/news/el-ni-o-puts-growing-pressure-on-rural-farmers-and-water-supplies/article_870a2c4e-f129-5c09-a6fb-6452a69e3e40.html)). Fiji says it now loses roughly $500 million a year to climate-related disasters ([Fiji Sun](https://fijisun.com.fj/news/nation/fiji-loses-500m-annually-to-climate-related-disasters)), and storm damage to Hawaii's infrastructure and schools is running into the hundreds of millions of dollars ([Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/09/21/hawaii-news/storm-damage-to-hawaii-infrastructure-and-schools-costing-hundreds-of-millions/)).

At the UN General Assembly, Pacific Islands Forum leaders pushed the US and World Bank for funding of the Pacific Resilience Facility and raised the region's priorities collectively ([PMN](https://pmn.co.nz/read/pacific-region/pacific-leaders-take-united-voice-to-un-general-assembly); [Pacific Islands Report roundup, 17–23 Sept](https://pireport.org/2026/09/24/pacific-islands-news-roundup-september-17-september-23-2026/)), while analysis this week warned that the broader UN funding crisis carries direct implications for Pacific humanitarian and development financing. Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale met UN Secretary-General António Guterres to press for greater climate finance, adaptation support and loss-and-damage funding. Separately, West Papuan advocates report six people killed in a new Indonesian security operation and are calling on Pacific leaders to raise the issue at UNGA ([Asia Pacific Report](https://asiapacificreport.nz/2026/09/24/wenda-calls-on-pacific-to-raise-west-papua-at-unga-after-six-killed-in-new-indonesian-massacre/)).

Regional disaster-response cooperation also advanced: Fiji has offered to host the newly developed Pacific Response Group, a mechanism for Pacific militaries to coordinate on future crises, and PNG's defence force and civilians continued disaster-relief training with the Wisconsin National Guard.

## 🌍 Tier 2 — Middle East & Africa

Sudan's crisis deepened on multiple fronts. A UN independent fact-finding mission concluded the RSF's siege and capture of El-Fasher bore "the hallmarks of genocide" ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/un-sudan-probe-finds-hallmarks-of-genocide-in-el-fasher/)), while roughly half a million people remain trapped in El-Obeid, North Kordofan, which the UN Human Rights Office warns could become the next site of a humanitarian catastrophe as RSF and allied forces mass around the city, striking power and fuel infrastructure and forcing hospitals and water stations offline. Nationally, an estimated 11.8 million people are displaced (7.4 million internally, 4.2 million in neighbouring countries), and WFP figures put 24.6 million people in acute hunger with roughly 2 million at famine or risk-of-famine levels ([WFP Sudan emergency page](https://www.wfp.org/emergencies/sudan)).

In the Occupied Palestinian Territory, WFP has been forced to halve food assistance in the West Bank — from 400,000 to 200,000 people — due to a funding shortfall, and needs an additional US$386 million over the next six months to serve roughly two million food-insecure people across Gaza and the West Bank ([WFP](https://www.wfp.org/news/funding-shortfall-forces-world-food-programme-cut-food-assistance-half-west-bank)). Aid truck volumes into Gaza fell 26 percent week-on-week (1,109 to 822) for 18–24 September, driven by fewer commercial deliveries, a near-halving of aid trucks, and additional Kerem Shalom closure days, even as UN Security Council briefers this week again demanded unhindered access.

Yemen's renewed fighting is displacing civilians at speed: UNICEF reports roughly 71,000 children forcibly displaced in the past three weeks, with more than a quarter of nearly 2,900 screened children acutely malnourished, and 243 schools closed or suspended, affecting close to 140,000 students ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/yemen-fighting-displaces-71000-children-worsens-malnutrition-unicef)). UNHCR separately warned that up to 10,000 people could flee across the Red Sea to Djibouti, with internal displacement potentially swelling past 230,000 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/22/un-warns-of-rising-yemen-displacement-as-thousands-flee-to-djibouti)).

Further south, the Democratic Republic of Congo's displacement crisis continues largely unabated: roughly 5.7 million people are internally displaced and over 1.2 million Congolese are refugees across Africa, against a backdrop of continued M23–government clashes in North and South Kivu and food insecurity affecting 28 million people nationally.

Underpinning all of the above, the ICRC disclosed a CHF 468 million funding shortfall for the remainder of 2026, and president Mirjana Spoljaric used this week's UNGA sidelines meeting to warn that "humanity is failing under our collective watch" on the safety of aid workers.

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Rest of the World

Global forced displacement stood at an estimated 117.8 million people by the end of 2025, and UNHCR's Barham Salih briefed the UN Security Council this month on the accelerating global displacement trend and funding cuts constraining the response ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168438)). In Somalia, WFP says it is reaching only around one in ten food-insecure people, with a US$147 million shortfall against a US$198 million requirement for the six months to February 2027. UNHCR's weekly Romania operational update (#214, 21 September) continues to track the Ukraine refugee situation in the region.

## 🚨 Sudden Onset Hazards

Typhoon Dujuan brought record-breaking rainfall to Japan's east coast, triggering evacuation orders for more than 1.9 million people on 21 September and knocking out power to over 50,000 households. In Vietnam, heavy rain and flooding since mid-September has affected more than 16,000 homes, with 3 fatalities, roughly 5,000 houses still inundated as of 21 September, and damage to 31 schools and close to 24,000 hectares of crops. Thailand, Indonesia and Malaysia also reported multi-province flooding and landslides this week (AHA Centre weekly disaster updates). Severe Tropical Storm Queenie (international name Surigae) tracked north-east past Taiwan through 26–27 September without directly affecting the Philippines, and was forecast to intensify into a typhoon near the Okinawa Islands before exiting the region. The magnitude 6.6–7 New Caledonia earthquake (26 September, see Tier 1) rounds out a week of overlapping hazards across the Asia-Pacific rim. Forecasters also flagged that El Niño is strengthening toward what could be an exceptional event extending into 2027, compounding food-security risk across Asia and the Pacific.
